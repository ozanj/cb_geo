## Abstract — staged, still pending

Your file now contains the proposed abstract under “PROPOSED,” alongside “SOE ABSTRACT,” inside an HTML comment. Choose/revise the version you want and make it active. The following is the existing proposal, not a newly approved replacement:

Geomarkets and the Market Segment Model were conceived from elite colleges’ shared understanding of stratified student markets. Geomarkets partition metropolitan space into proprietary recruiting territories. College Board commercialized and circulated these classifications through its recruiting products, making them available to colleges beyond the originating network. Student Search Service sells prospective student contact information—or “student lists”—which colleges filter by Geomarket to determine which prospect profiles to purchase. Using concepts from the market devices literature, this manuscript investigates how Geomarket filtering within Student Search Service can shape the racial/ethnic and first-generation composition of academically similar prospect pools. We analyze actual student lists purchased by public universities, obtained through public records requests. Holding academic criteria constant, our analyses vary the Geomarket filter to show how different filter choices would change the composition of the visible prospect pool. Geomarkets partition academically similar prospects into racially and socioeconomically distinct pools. Filtering toward affluent Geomarkets and away from poorer ones can disproportionately exclude Black, Hispanic, and first-generation Asian students from colleges’ prospect pools. These analyses contribute to college-access scholarship by moving inquiry upstream from observable college behavior to the third-party products that make particular forms of organizational behavior possible.

**Student list products**. Lead generation is the process of converting "prospects" -- consumers a business wants to target -- into "leads" -- prospects whose contact information has been obtained [@ftc2016]. In enrollment management, "student lists" (also called "names") are spreadsheets that contain the contact information of prospects [@belkin2019-studata]. Student lists are a fundamental input for undergraduate recruiting campaigns because purchased names -- alongside prospects who reach out on their own -- constitute the set of prospects who receive subsequent recruiting interventions (e.g., mail, email) designed to push them to apply and enroll. Student list products are "selection devices" -- routines for making decisions about who receives an intervention [@RN4778] -- that enable colleges to select which names they purchase by incorporating search filters (e.g., HS graduating class, state, test score). Historically, the largest student list vendors are College Board and ACT, which create student list products based on their database of test-takers.
